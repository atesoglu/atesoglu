# Interviewing Guide

A practical guide to preparing for engineering interviews, telling your story clearly, and evaluating the company while you interview.

## 1. Prepare Deliberately

Success depends on deliberate practice across coding, system design, and behavioral questions. Companies vary in how they level roles, so understand the expectations for the specific position before you start.

### Coding Interviews

**Assessment rubric:** Technical skill, problem-solving, and culture-fit are evaluated simultaneously.

**Common mistakes:** Skipping clarification or failing to check in with the interviewer.

**Topics to practise:** String manipulation, arrays and algorithms, KMP, binary search, and inflection-point problems.

### System Design Interviews

Focus on clarifying requirements, articulating trade-offs, and designing collaboratively. Core concepts include load balancing, consistency, URL shorteners, distributed LRU caches, and message queues.

### Behavioral Interviews

Use the STAR method. Prepare examples for failure, conflict, and leadership.

## 2. Tell Your Story

Use a repeatable two-minute pitch built from three parts:

1. **Most relevant results:** "I have been [role] for [time]... Recently I [outcome] which helped [company result]."
2. **Your biggest strength:** Describe one strength and how you apply it.
3. **Your one ask:** What you want from the role and the team, expressed as a short closing question.

**Example:** "I have been in digital marketing for 10 years... Recently I managed a campaign that generated 500+ new leads... My main strength is creating content on LinkedIn... I'm looking for a team that values a get-it-done attitude."

## 3. Answer Common Questions

Walk through your background, highlight key accomplishments and career moves, and end by explaining what you want next and why you are looking.

**Example:** "I started my career in Marketing after graduating with a Business degree in 2011. I've spent my entire career at Google, receiving 3 promotions and 4 awards for outstanding performance. I'm looking to join a smaller company now and take on more leadership and project management."

### Questions to Prepare For

1. Why do you want to leave your current job? — Focus on growth, not criticism.
2. Why do you want to work here? — Show alignment with company values.
3. What are your 3 biggest weaknesses? — Be honest and show improvement.
4. Tell me your story. — Concise, milestone-focused narrative.
5. What are your salary expectations? — Ask for their range first.
6. What makes you excited about work? — Align with company mission.
7. What sets you apart from other candidates? — Use data and specifics.
8. What's the hardest problem you've worked on? — Explain the problem and outcome.
9. What's the achievement you're most proud of? — Tie it to impact.
10. What's the best piece of feedback you've received? — Show growth.
11. Tell me about a time you missed a deadline. — Own it and show remediation.
12. Describe a hard decision you made under pressure. — Explain the process and trade-offs.

## 4. Questions to Ask the Interviewer

### High-Signal Questions

- What's one thing you'd change about the company? (reveals real challenges)
- What do your top performers have in common? (reveals success traits)
- What's your proudest moment working here? (reveals celebrated values)
- How do you motivate your team during tough times? (reveals leadership quality)
- How do you recognize and celebrate wins? (reveals retention and morale practices)
- How do people structure their workweek? (reveals flexibility)
- What does success in this role look like in the first 90 days? (sets clear expectations)

### Company

- What do you and other employees like most about working here?
- How would you describe the corporate culture? What are the company's goals for the future?
- What is the overall style of management? What kind of leadership style do you promote?
- What, if any, are your plans for transitioning from remote to in-person work?
- How does the company take an idea from inception to completion?
- How does the innovation process work? Is it "move fast and break things" or precision and artistry?
- How much time is spent on creating new products and projects?
- What model of reinforcement do you use to correct and instruct? How is feedback given to employees?

### People

- How many people have joined the company recently?
- How many people have left the company in the last year?
- Are there opportunities for professional development? If so, what do those look like?

### Team

- Who will I be working most closely with? Could you tell me a little about the person I would report to directly?
- What processes and technology do you use to collaborate?
- May I ask why the last person left the job?

### Role

- What are the primary responsibilities of the position? What is the most challenging aspect of the job?
- What do the day-to-day responsibilities look like?
- What does success look like in this position, and how is it measured?
- What would I be expected to accomplish in my first month and first year?
- Do you expect the responsibilities for this role to change in the near future?
- How does senior management view and interact with the person in this position?
- How does the position fit in relation to the rest of the organization?
- Is there a career path that someone in this position would be expected to follow?
- How well do people with my background adjust to working here?
- What does the performance-review process look like?
- Are there any special projects I would work on soon?
- How is information documented and shared across projects and departments?
- What would I need to achieve to advance within the position and company?
- What are the current focus areas for the company as a whole?
- How do you reward employees for good work?
- Do you see the role expanding in the future?
- Do you have any on-the-job training?

### Additional Questions

- Is the Big Hairy Audacious Goal (BHAG) authentic, and has the company laid out a credible path to it?
- How competitive is the market? Are there rival companies in the same business?
- Can you tell me more about the company's DNA and future direction?
- What is the company's background in this domain, and how did it get started?

### Technical Challenges and Ownership

- What are the team's main technical challenges over the next 12–18 months?
- How much ownership will I have over architecture and technical direction?
- How does the team balance new product development with operational support?
- What does success look like after six months?

### Questions to Avoid

- Instead of "What's the salary?" ask "What does success look like in the first 90 days?"
- Instead of "What's the day-to-day?" ask "Which key initiatives would I own in Q1?"
- Instead of "What's the biggest challenge?" ask "What key obstacles hold the top team members back?"
- Instead of "How's the team culture?" ask for a concrete example of collaboration.
