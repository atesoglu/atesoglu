# Senior Engineering Manager

The designer of the engineering "engine." Accountable for the health, growth and output of multiple teams and their managers.

## Role Expectations

### 1. Core Accountability
The Senior EM (or Manager of Managers) ensures that their managers are effective leaders, their teams are aligned with business priorities and the organizational "machinery" (hiring, growth, process) is running smoothly.

### 2. Impact Areas

#### 👥 Leadership Development (Primary Focus)
- **Managing Managers**: Coaches EMs on performance management, delegation and coaching.
- **Leadership Pipeline**: Identifies and grooms the next generation of leads and principals.
- **Culture Scaling**: Ensures that cultural values (e.g., "blameless culture") are maintained across multiple teams.

#### 📈 Operational Strategy
- **Resource Allocation**: Manages headcount and identifies where to invest (or divest) engineering resources.
- **Process Harmonization**: Ensures consistency across teams without killing local team autonomy.
- **Departmental DORA**: Accountable for the aggregate metrics and velocity of the entire domain.

#### 🤝 Strategic Alignment
- **Product Partnership**: Collaborates with Director-level product and design peers to define the domain-level roadmap.
- **Risk Mitigation**: Proactively identifies large-scale technical or people risks (e.g., critical skill silos).

## First 30 Days

*Transitioning from "Team Leader" to "Organizational System Designer."*

### 1. Days 1–10: The Manager-of-Managers Transition
- [ ] **Skip-Level 1:1s**: Meet the individual contributors reporting to your EMs. Listen for gaps between management vision and day-to-day reality.
- [ ] **Manager 1:1s**: Understand the challenges of your direct report EMs. Are they struggling with delegation? Do they have clear goals?
- [ ] **Resource Audit**: Review the budget, headcount and hiring plans for your entire department/domain.

### 2. Days 11–20: Identifying Departmental Friction
- [ ] **Cross-Team Dependencies**: Map the most common blockers between your teams and other departments.
- [ ] **Standardization Check**: Are your teams using wildly different processes for CRs, deployments, or on-call? Identify where consistency is needed.
- [ ] **Business Alignment**: Sync with the Product Director to ensure the department-wide roadmap is technically feasible.

### 3. Days 21–30: Developing the "Manager Backbone"
- [ ] **Define Management Rituals**: Establish a "Managers Forum" or sync for your leads to share best practices.
- [ ] **Identify "High-Potentials"**: Who is the next EM or Principal in your organization? Start sponsorship plans.
- [ ] **Define Department Metrics**: Move beyond team-level DORA metrics to aggregate health and throughput indicators for the whole domain.

### 4. Key Behavioral Shift
- **Lead Through Leads**: Your primary output is now the *effectiveness of your managers*. Resist the urge to fix team-level problems directly.
- **Focus on the "Whitespaces"**: Spend your time on the problems that fall between teams (inter-team dependencies).

## Long-Term Success

*Building a high-performance "organization of teams" over 6-12 months.*

### 1. The 12-Month Roadmap

- **3–6 Months**: **Management Maturity**. Establish clear career ladders and growth frameworks across your domain. Ensure all EMs are performing consistent 1:1s and feedback loops.
- **6–12 Months**: **Operational Scaling**. Implement cross-team standards for reliability and security. Reduce "waste" in inter-team handoffs by 20%.
- **1 Year+**: **Strategic Resilience**. Build an organization that can absorb the loss of a key lead or EM without losing momentum. Focus on external hiring brand and leadership pipeline.

### 2. Success, Failure and Recovery

| The Path to Success | The Path to Failure | The Crisis Recovery |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Empowerment**: You give EMs the resources and safety to fail and learn. | **The Shadow EM**: You continue managing teams directly, bypassing your own leads. | **The "Leadership Pivot"**: You re-assign a struggling lead to an IC role with dignity. |
| **Systems Thinking**: You fix the *organizational* flaws that lead to team burnout. | **Metric Obsession**: You demand "higher velocity" without fixing underlying friction. | **Departmental Reset**: You pause a project to fix a cultural or technical rot in the domain. |

### 3. The Promotion Path: Toward Director of Engineering
To move to the Director level (Business-Unit Leader), you must demonstrate:
- **Business Impact**: You've led a technical initiative that directly resulted in measurable revenue growth or cost savings.
- **Organizational Design**: You've successfully restructuring or built a new function (e.g., Platform Engineering) within your domain.
- **Executive Diplomacy**: You are a trusted partner to Product, Design and Sales VPs.
- **Culture of Quality**: Your domain has zero "high-severity" recurring incidents and top-tier DORA rankings.
