# Director of Engineering

The steward of a Business Unit's engineering strategy. Accountable for the intersection of technology, people and business value for a major segment of the company.

## Role Expectations

### 1. Core Accountability
The Director of Engineering ensures their department is healthy, the technical strategy enables business goals and that they are building a sustainable leadership bench. They focus on the *what* and the *why* at a multi-department scale.

### 2. Impact Areas

#### 🏦 Business & Strategy (Primary Focus)
- **Roadmap Alignment**: Ensures the engineering work directly supports the 1-year business strategy.
- **Financial Ownership**: Manages the budget for their unit, including third-party tools and cloud costs.
- **Portfolio Management**: Decides where to invest, maintain, or kill various internal products/services.

#### 👥 Organizational Health
- **Manager Coaching**: Coaches Senior EMs on organizational design, headcount planning and complex performance issues.
- **Retention & Growth**: Accountable for the attrition and growth metrics of a 50-100+ person organization.
- **Hiring Strategy**: Defines the long-term hiring needs and shapes the technical interview standards for the company.

#### ⚙️ Operational Excellence
- **Global DORA Metrics**: Ensures the Business Unit meets target DORA scores and reliability standards.
- **Platform Strategy**: Collaborates with Architects to define the "Golden Path" that speeds up development across their teams.

## First 30 Days

*Transitioning from "Operations Manager" to "Business Unit Partner."*

### 1. Days 1–10: The Broad Multi-Department View
- [ ] **VP / Executive Syncs**: Meet your VP of Engineering and C-suite peers. Understand the 1-year business goals and current "Board-level" concerns.
- [ ] **The "Unit" Health Audit**: Review the attrition rates, employee engagement (e.g., eNPS) and delivery health of all departments under your remit.
- [ ] **Stakeholder "Pain Point" Tour**: Meet with Sales, Support and Customer Success. Ask: "What are we building that makes your life easier and what are we doing that makes it harder?"

### 2. Days 11–20: Analyzing the Strategy-Execution Gap
- [ ] **Roadmap Transparency**: Is the company strategy actually reflected in the team backlogs? Identify where "disconnected" work is happening.
- [ ] **Tech-Debt Balance**: Are we investing enough in platform stability to support the 12-month growth plan? 
- [ ] **Manager Review**: Evaluate the strength of your Senior EM bench. Who are your "lieutenants"?

### 3. Days 21–30: The Director-Level Vision
- [ ] **Draft the Engineering Strategy**: Define the 3-5 key technical pillars for your business unit for the next 2 quarters.
- [ ] **Establish "Pulse" Rituals**: Move from participating in plannings to reviewing department-wide "Quarterly Business Reviews" (QBRs) for engineering.
- [ ] **Define the "Win"**: What is the one thing your business unit must achieve this quarter to be considered successful?

### 4. Key Behavioral Shift
- **Think in Quarters, not Sprints**: Your time horizon must expand significantly.
- **Impact via Policy**: You change things by changing *policies*, *budgets* and *organizational structures*, not by fixing processes.

## Long-Term Success

*Driving business outcomes through organizational engineering excellence over 6-12 months.*

### 1. The 12-Month Roadmap

- **3–6 Months**: **Strategic Realignment**. Ensure every engineering hour spent is defensible against business objectives. Implement clear "Buy vs Build" decision frameworks.
- **6–12 Months**: **Cultural Transformation**. Drive "Product-Minded Engineering" across the department. Build a brand of "Invisible Quality" where systems just work.
- **1 Year+**: **Organizational Innovation**. Introduce new ways of working (e.g., internal open-source, incubator teams) that significantly accelerate the company's innovation rate.

### 2. Success, Failure and Recovery

| The Path to Success | The Path to Failure | The Crisis Recovery |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Business Value First**: You prioritize technical strategy based on market needs. | **The Tech Purist**: You favor technical "purity" even if it kills the company's go-to-market speed. | **The Strategic Trim**: You lead the difficult decision to kill an underperforming project to save resources. |
| **Sponsorship of Directors**: You grow your SEMs into Future Directors. | **The Bottleneck**: You insist on approving every significant architectural or hiring decision. | **The Quality Intervention**: You halt all feature development to restore a broken system trust with customers. |

### 3. The Promotion Path: Toward VP of Engineering
To move to the VP level (Functional Leader), you must demonstrate:
- **Global Strategy**: You've influenced the direction of the *entire* engineering organization, not just your unit.
- **Executive Influence**: You are the "technical conscious" for the CEO or CTO.
- **Operational Scalability**: You've implemented systems that allowed the engineering org to double in size with minimal loss in efficiency.
- **Talent Brand**: You've made the company a "destination" for top-tier talent through your leadership and public presence.
