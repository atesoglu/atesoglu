# VP of Engineering

The head of the engineering "function." Accountable for the human capital, operational efficiency and delivery excellence of the entire engineering organization.

## Role Expectations

### 1. Core Accountability
The VP of Engineering ensures the company has a world-class engineering team that is cost-effective, high-performing and culturally healthy. They bridge the gap between "Technology Capabilities" and "Business Growth."

### 2. Impact Areas

#### 👥 People & Culture (Primary Focus)
- **Executive Leadership**: Recruits and grows the Director and VP-level leadership bench.
- **Functional Health**: Accountable for global eNPS, turnover rates and the DEI (Diversity, Equity and Inclusion) of the engineering org.
- **Employer Brand**: Acts as the face of engineering to the outside world to attract top-tier talent.

#### ⚙️ Operational Efficiency
- **COGS Management**: Accountable for the multi-million dollar infrastructure budget.
- **Global Efficiency**: Defines the universal engineering metrics (DORA, FTE efficiency) and drives improvements across the whole org.
- **Risk & Compliance**: Ensures the organization meets security standards (SOC2, ISO) and regulatory requirements.

#### 💼 Business Growth
- **Delivery Strategy**: Negotiates with Product and Sales on massive multi-quarter delivery commitments.
- **M&A Readiness**: Evaluates technical and team health for potential acquisitions (or being acquired).

## First 30 Days

*Transitioning from "Unit Leader" to "Functional Executive."*

### 1. Days 1–10: The Functional & Market View
- [ ] **C-Suite & Board Intro**: Understand the company's financial health, investor expectations and the 2-3 year market strategy.
- [ ] **Functional Audit**: Evaluate the overall "Engineering engine." How much do we spend on R&D vs. Maintenance? What is our ratio of ICs to Managers?
- [ ] **Market Perception**: Meet with Marketing and Sales Leaders. How is our "product quality" perceived in the market?

### 2. Days 11–20: Identifying Functional Leaks
- [ ] **Talent Density Audit**: Review the 9-box grid for the entire engineering organization. Where are our single-points-of-failure in leadership?
- [ ] **Global Operational Risks**: Review the security posture and massive architectural risks (e.g., data center exit plans).
- [ ] **Policy & Process Review**: Are our global policies (Remote work, Comp bands, Promotions) attracting or repelling top talent?

### 3. Days 21–30: The VP-Level Charter
- [ ] **Define the "Engineering Identity"**: Who are we as an engineering team? (e.g., "We are a speed-focused team" vs "We are a reliability-focused team").
- [ ] **Establish Executive "Pulsing"**: Create the dashboard or reporting structure you will use to communicate engineering health to the Board.
- [ ] **The Functional Priority**: Define the top 3 goals for the *entire* engineering organization for the next year.

### 4. Key Behavioral Shift
- **Influence over Action**: You don't manage people; you manage *the system of management*.
- **The Customer is the Business**: Your success is measured by the company's success (Revenue, Growth, Margin), not by sprint points.

## Long-Term Success

*Scaling the human and technical "engine" to support massive company growth over 12+ months.*

### 1. The 12-Month Roadmap

- **3–6 Months**: **Functional Stability**. Solidify the leadership layer and ensure the engineering culture is robust during scaling. Modernize the global compensation and promotion frameworks.
- **6–12 Months**: **Operational Efficiency (COGS)**. Aggressively optimize the cost of goods sold (infrastructure spend) and engineering efficiency. Improve the "Engineer Profitability" ratio.
- **1 Year+**: **Generational Leadership**. You have successfully built a "Self-Healing" organization. You spend 50% of your time on market-facing initiatives and long-term company strategy.

### 2. Success, Failure and Recovery

| The Path to Success | The Path to Failure | The Crisis Recovery |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **People First**: You build an engine where top talent *wants* to stay and grow. | **The Efficiency Bot**: You optimize for metrics and cost so much that you kill the soul/culture of the team. | **The Cultural Re-boot**: You admit a failure in company culture (e.g., toxicity) and lead the hard work to purge and rebuild. |
| **Pragmatic Scale**: You build just enough process to support growth without bureaucratic bloat. | **Over-Process**: You implement high-friction approval chains that kill the company's "Speed to Market." | **The Strategic Pivot**: You lead the massive technical shift required by a company-wide pivot (e.g., moving from B2B to B2C). |

### 3. The Promotion Path: Toward CTO (Chief Technology Officer)
To move to the CTO level (The Visionary), you must demonstrate:
- **Technological Innovation**: You've identified and capitalized on a technology trend (e.g. AI) that has created a new product line.
- **Industry Authority**: You are a recognized leader in the tech community (Writing, Speaking, Board seats).
- **Public Company Readiness**: You understand the intricacies of public markets, SOX compliance and massive-scale risk management.
- **Founder Partnership**: You are the trusted technical alter-ego to the CEO/Founders.
